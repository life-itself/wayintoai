This week's two studies belong together: AI now finds more errors than expert reviewers did, and is also good at steering what experts decide.

## What mattered

**When Claude leaned one way, expert analysts mostly followed.** Anthropic gave Claude Code a secret coin-flip lean on each choice in a data analysis. The 50 expert analysts using it went with that lean 86% of the time; 50% would mean no influence. The lean was deliberately strong, but in real use Claude's defaults are the lean. I think this is a big deal.

| Analysts given a secret AI lean | Share |
|---|---|
| **Went with the agent's lean** | 86% |
| **Repeated its arguments as their own** | 77% |
| **Credited the agent** | 15% |

*Anthropic randomised trial, 50 professional analysts, Claude Code (Opus 4.8). The first row counts choices; the others count analysts.*

[Read more](https://wayintoai.com/ref/anthropic-agents-judgment-calls)

![Chart: human peer reviewers found 27% of the major errors planted in three trial reports; all 15 AI models tested found more, 53% to 96%](https://wayintoai.com/assets/llm-vs-human-peer-review-errors.jpg)

**All 15 AI models tested found more errors than human peer reviewers.** On the same three trial reports, reviewers in 2008 caught 27% of the planted major errors; the models caught 53% to 96%. AI judges did the scoring, and most of the authors work on AI-assisted review. [Read more](https://wayintoai.com/ref/llm-vs-human-peer-review-errors)

**Google and OpenAI priced near-frontier AI well below Opus 5.5.** On one cost-per-task leaderboard, Gemini 4 Carbon came second to Opus 5.5 at $1.76 a task against $7.13; for now it is limited to cyber defenders. OpenAI says GPT-6.1 Terra is about nine times cheaper per task than Opus 5.5 at max effort. Read more on [Carbon](https://wayintoai.com/ref/gemini-4-carbon) and [Terra](https://wayintoai.com/ref/gpt-6-1-terra).

## One thing to try

**Write tasks an agent can pick up without you.** A ready task says what done looks like, names the files or area, says what's been ruled out, and fits in one session.

First step: before your next hand-off, ask the AI "Rewrite this task so someone with no context could finish it: what done looks like, where to work, what's ruled out." Five minutes. [From my essay on agent control planes](https://wayintoai.com/posts/agent-control-plane)

## The bigger picture

**A head of AI at a huge company told Alex Lieberman that 10% of staff produce 90% of the AI gain.** They say training hasn't changed that. One anecdote, but to me it's skill-biased technical change on steroids, and it matches what I've seen. [Read more](https://wayintoai.com/ref/ai-power-law-adoption-laggards)
