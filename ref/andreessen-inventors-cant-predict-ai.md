# Andreessen: AI's inventors are the worst people to ask about its future

**A viral clip of Marc Andreessen argues that inventors can't predict how their inventions will be used (Edison and the phonograph), so Geoffrey Hinton's warnings about AI deserve no special weight.**

> **Rufus's note (2026-11-22):**  
> This kind of argument is worth rebutting systematically. It is true that we overweight authorities, so we should look at the logic of the argument.

## Links

- **Post with clip**: [Konstantinos Chasiotis on X](https://x.com/KonWrites/status/2124526941722001619), 22 Nov 2026

## The argument

- People defer to Hinton because he helped invent modern AI, and he predicts "rampant unemployment" and UBI.
- Edison thought the phonograph would be used for recorded sermons; it was used for music. Inventors are "too buried in the specifics of the here and now".
- So nobody in the lab, Andreessen included, can forecast AI's economic or social effects.

The post itself goes on to use this as a pitch for building a personal brand.

## Points for a rebuttal

- It cuts both ways: if no one can predict, that includes the confident optimists, and deep uncertainty about a powerful technology is a reason for caution, not for ignoring warnings.
- Hinton's core warning is not a forecast of uses (like Edison's sermons). It is about control: what happens when systems get more capable than us. That is a claim about the technology itself, where builders do know more.
- Many warnings now come from many independent sources, not one inventor. See [[moc-ai-risk-warnings]].
- Andreessen is an investor with a large stake in AI; dismissing authority should apply to his authority too.
