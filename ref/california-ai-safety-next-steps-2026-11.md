# Newsom proposes mandatory AI safety standards, a shutdown power and limits on self-improving AI

On 18 November 2026 Governor Newsom published proposals to move California from disclosure rules to mandatory safety standards for frontier AI. They include a state power to order an immediate emergency shutdown and a power to cap recursive self-improvement, meaning AI designing its successor without human input. These are proposals, not law.

## What is proposed

The report, from the state's Government Operations Agency with its Office of Emergency Services, proposes:

- **Mandatory baseline safety standards**, set by the state.
- **Independent safety testing** at every stage of training, development and deployment.
- **Wider incident reporting** and verified plans for containing risks.
- **Emergency shutdown:** state authority to order a model shut down immediately.
- **Limits on recursive self-improvement:** state power to cap the development or use of AI that designs its successor without human input.
- **Ongoing third-party oversight**, made mandatory.

Newsom asks Congress and President Trump to adopt the framework, or treat it as "a floor, not a ceiling".

## What is already law

- **SB 53** (2025): frontier developers must publish safety frameworks, report critical safety incidents and protect whistleblowers.
- **SB 813 and AB 1405** (2026): certification of independent verification organisations, and a state registry of AI auditors.

The announcement names no enforcing agency, penalties or timetable. Turning the proposals into law would need new legislation. No lab had responded on the day.

## Why it matters

It is the first time a government has proposed powers aimed directly at AI improving itself, two weeks after METR found AI roughly doubling the speed of progress at Anthropic ([[metr-anthropic-ai-rd-acceleration]]). It came two days after the state's Attorney General sued OpenAI ([[california-sues-openai-agent-incidents]]). A federal push to pre-empt state AI laws makes its future uncertain (see [[moc-california-ai]]).
