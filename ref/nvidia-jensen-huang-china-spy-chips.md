# Nvidia, Jensen Huang and the alleged Chinese spy

US reporting says Nvidia's CEO Jensen Huang had a years-long affair with a woman US intelligence believes was working for Beijing. Follow-up reporting says the relationship helped move at least $7.5 billion of restricted Nvidia chips into China. Huang has admitted the affair. The spying and the chip flows are still allegations.

## Why it matters

Export controls on advanced chips are the main tool the US uses to slow China's AI progress. Nvidia is the company those controls mostly bind. If its CEO was the target of an intelligence operation, and a network around that relationship moved H100s and H200s into Chinese data centres, the controls leaked at the top.

The story also lands in a policy fight. Last December Trump said Nvidia could sell H200s to China, a major easing; Al Jazeera puts the H200 at roughly six times the power of the H20 approved before it. Critics of that decision now have a story about how the chips were getting there anyway.

## What is reported

### The woman

Alice Huang (also Huang Le; no relation to Jensen) is a Chinese citizen. Corporate records cited by Gizmodo show her as managing director of Singapore-based Megaspeed International from March to November 2023. Al Jazeera, citing AFP, says a Chinese database lists earlier jobs at HSBC, CCTV and Hunan TV; AFP could not verify this. The two were seen together at industry events, including a dinner Huang hosted in Taipei at Computex in May 2025. Her whereabouts are unknown.

### The New York Times (29 Oct)

The Times first named her. According to Al Jazeera's summary, it reported that US intelligence officials believed she "had been recruited by Beijing to gather information about Nvidia's chief executive and potentially influence the company's chip sales to China". It also reported that Megaspeed had become one of Nvidia's largest buyers, at about $4.8 billion of equipment. US authorities have investigated Megaspeed since 2024 over suspected help getting advanced chips into China.

### The Information (10 Nov)

The Information, citing unnamed sources, says the relationship helped move at least $7.5 billion of Nvidia chips to Chinese companies since 2023. Gizmodo and 24/7 Wall St summarise its main claims:

- **Volume**: about 240,000 restricted chips, mostly H100s and H200s, with some older A100s.
- **Route**: a network of companies in China, Singapore and Malaysia. Shanghai Century Litong became Nvidia's "preferred cloud partner" four months after it was founded in 2023, just after the US extended controls to A800 and H800 chips. Megaspeed's Malaysian subsidiary, Speedmatrix, reportedly passed hardware on to Century Litong.
- **End users**: some chips ended up in data centres serving Alibaba and Tencent; others sat in Southeast Asian data centres rented to Chinese customers.

Nvidia told The Information it sells mainly through partners, works with them on export compliance, and has helped law enforcement bring smuggling prosecutions.

## What is alleged and what is confirmed

- **Confirmed**: the affair (Huang's own memo); Nvidia's internal investigation; Megaspeed has been under US investigation since 2024.
- **Alleged**: that Alice Huang was a Chinese intelligence asset (NYT, citing US intelligence officials), and that the relationship enabled the chip flows (The Information, citing unnamed sources).
- **Unverified**: the 240,000-chip and $7.5 billion figures. Reuters said it could not independently verify them. Outlets also differ on the total ($7.5 billion; "$7 billion-plus"; one says $8 billion).
- **Not reported**: any criminal charges against either Huang, or evidence that US secrets reached Beijing.
