# Nvidia, Jensen Huang and the alleged Chinese spy

US reporting says Nvidia's CEO Jensen Huang had an affair with a woman US intelligence reportedly believes was working for Beijing. Follow-up reporting says the relationship helped move at least $7.5 billion of restricted Nvidia chips to Chinese companies. Neither of them has been charged. Huang has admitted the affair. The spying and the chip flows are still allegations.

> **Rufus's note (2026-11-11):** The CEO of a $4.5 trillion company, the one that makes the chips the AI race runs on, admits an affair with a woman US intelligence reportedly believes was working for Beijing. You couldn't make this up. The ramifications go well beyond AI. It has already knocked about $190 billion off Nvidia's value in a day, and Nvidia's board is investigating whether its CEO was targeted by foreign intelligence. It raises questions for the Trump administration: did it fail to act when it should have, simply get it wrong, or allow things it shouldn't have? And it shows how badly people want these chips, and how long-term China's strategic thinking is. At one point or another, all of these stories showed up in [[ai-2027]], even the espionage.

## Why it matters

Export controls on advanced chips are the main tool the US uses to slow China's AI progress. Nvidia is the company those controls mostly bind. If its CEO was the target of an intelligence operation, and a network around that relationship moved restricted H100s and H200s to Chinese customers, the controls leaked at the top.

It is also a political story beyond AI. Trump, who reportedly received an intelligence briefing, was asked whether he should have acted: "No, I don't think so." 24/7 Wall St reports him saying he believes Huang didn't know she was a spy.

Markets took it seriously. Nvidia lost about $190 billion of market value on Monday 9 November, according to 24/7 Wall St, and fell again on Tuesday. Bank of America cautioned: "We would not equate allegations with proven business damage."

It also lands in a policy fight. In December 2025 Trump said Nvidia could sell H200s to China, a major easing; Al Jazeera puts the H200 at roughly six times the power of the H20. So not every H200 in China after that is illicit. But the reported flows started in 2023, under the stricter rules.

## What is reported

### The woman

Alice Huang (also Huang Le; no relation to Jensen) is a Chinese citizen. Corporate records cited by Gizmodo show her as managing director of Singapore-based Megaspeed International from March to November 2023. Al Jazeera, citing AFP, says a Chinese database lists earlier jobs at HSBC, CCTV and Hunan TV; AFP could not verify this.

The two were seen together at industry events, including a dinner Huang hosted in Taipei at Computex in May 2025. Her whereabouts are unknown, and she has not publicly responded.

### The New York Times (29 Oct)

The Times first named her. According to Al Jazeera's summary, it reported that US intelligence officials believed she "had been recruited by Beijing to gather information about Nvidia's chief executive", and potentially to influence its chip sales to China.

The Times also reported that Megaspeed had become one of Nvidia's largest buyers, at about $4.8 billion of equipment. Bloomberg had earlier put the figure at $4.6 billion. US authorities have reportedly investigated Megaspeed since 2024 over suspected help getting advanced chips into China, and Gizmodo says Singapore has investigated it too.

### The Information (10 Nov)

The Information, citing unnamed sources, says the relationship helped move at least $7.5 billion of Nvidia chips to Chinese companies since 2023. Gizmodo and 24/7 Wall St summarise its main claims:

- **Volume**: about 240,000 restricted chips, mostly H100s and H200s, with some older A100s.
- **Route**: a network of companies in China, Singapore and Malaysia. Shanghai Century Litong became Nvidia's "preferred cloud partner" four months after it was founded in 2023, just after the US extended controls to A800 and H800 chips. Gizmodo says its founding shareholder, which exited in 2024, was indirectly controlled by Alice Huang's sister. Some hardware from Megaspeed's Malaysian subsidiary, Speedmatrix, reportedly ended up with Century Litong.
- **End users**: some chips ended up in data centres serving Alibaba and Tencent; others sat in Southeast Asian data centres rented to Chinese customers.

Nvidia told The Information it sells mainly through partners, works with them on export compliance, and has helped law enforcement bring smuggling prosecutions.

## What is alleged and what is confirmed

- **Confirmed**: the affair (Huang's own memo); Nvidia's board investigation (its own statement).
- **Reported**: that Megaspeed has been under US investigation since 2024 (NYT, per Al Jazeera).
- **Alleged**: that Alice Huang was a Chinese intelligence asset (NYT, citing US intelligence officials), and that the relationship enabled the chip flows (The Information, citing unnamed sources).
- **Unverified**: the 240,000-chip and $7.5 billion figures. Reuters said it could not independently verify them. Outlets also differ on the total ($7.5 billion; "$7 billion-plus"; one says $8 billion).
- **Not reported**: any criminal charges against either Huang, or evidence that US secrets reached Beijing.
